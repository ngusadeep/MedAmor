# Medication Administration Standard Operating Procedure

**Version:** 2.3
**Last Updated:** January 26, 2026
**Effective Date:** February 1, 2026
**Review Date:** July 2026

## Purpose
To ensure safe, accurate, and timely medication administration to all patients.

## Scope
Applies to all medication administration by licensed healthcare providers.

## General Principles

### Five Rights of Medication Administration
1. **Right Patient** - Verify patient identity
2. **Right Medication** - Confirm drug name and form
3. **Right Dose** - Verify dosage calculation
4. **Right Route** - Confirm administration route
5. **Right Time** - Check timing and frequency

### Additional Safety Checks
- Right documentation
- Right reason/indication
- Right response monitoring

## Preparation Phase

### Medication Order Review
- Verify order completeness and legibility
- Check for allergies and contraindications
- Confirm drug interactions
- Validate dosing appropriateness

### Preparation Procedures
- Calculate dosages accurately
- Prepare medications in designated area
- Label medications appropriately
- Verify against original order

## Administration Routes

### Oral Medications
- Verify patient ability to swallow
- Provide with adequate water
- Monitor for proper ingestion
- Document administration

### Intravenous Medications
- Verify line patency and placement
- Check for signs of infiltration
- Administer at proper rate
- Monitor for adverse reactions

### Subcutaneous/Intramuscular Injections
- Select appropriate site
- Clean site with antiseptic
- Use proper technique
- Apply pressure post-injection

## Documentation Requirements

### Medication Administration Record (MAR)
- Date and time of administration
- Dose administered
- Route of administration
- Initials of administering nurse
- Patient response documentation

### Incident Reporting
- Medication errors
- Adverse drug reactions
- Near-miss events
- Corrective actions taken

## High-Risk Medications

### Insulin Administration
- Double-check calculations
- Verify blood glucose levels
- Monitor for hypoglycemia signs
- Document site rotation

### Chemotherapy Agents
- Independent double-checks
- Personal protective equipment
- Spill kit availability
- Waste disposal protocols

### Controlled Substances
- Witnessed administration
- Count verification
- Waste documentation
- Diversion prevention

## Patient Education

### Medication Teaching
- Drug name and purpose
- Proper administration technique
- Common side effects
- When to contact healthcare provider

### Discharge Counseling
- Medication reconciliation
- Written instructions provision
- Follow-up appointment scheduling
- Pharmacy contact information

## Quality Assurance

### Monitoring and Auditing
- Random medication cart audits
- Administration documentation review
- Error rate tracking
- Performance improvement initiatives

### Staff Competency
- Initial medication administration training
- Annual competency validation
- Continuing education requirements
- Skills assessment

## Emergency Procedures

### Adverse Drug Reactions
- Immediate discontinuation
- Vital signs monitoring
- Emergency medication preparation
- Physician notification

### Medication Errors
- Assess patient condition
- Implement corrective measures
- Document incident thoroughly
- Report according to policy
