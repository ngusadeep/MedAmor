# Hand Hygiene Standard Operating Procedure

**Version:** 1.4
**Last Updated:** January 26, 2026
**Effective Date:** February 1, 2026
**Review Date:** January 2027

## Purpose
To prevent healthcare-associated infections through proper hand hygiene practices.

## Scope
Applies to all healthcare personnel, patients, and visitors in all clinical areas.

## Hand Hygiene Indications

### When to Perform Hand Hygiene
- Before touching a patient
- Before clean/aseptic procedures
- After body fluid exposure risk
- After touching patient surroundings
- After removing gloves

### Additional Indications
- Before eating or handling food
- After using the restroom
- After coughing, sneezing, or blowing nose
- After touching high-touch surfaces
- Upon entering and exiting patient rooms

## Hand Hygiene Methods

### Alcohol-Based Hand Rub (Preferred Method)
1. Apply product to palm of one hand
2. Rub hands together covering all surfaces
3. Rub until hands are dry (15-30 seconds)
4. Avoid touching surfaces until dry

### Soap and Water (When Required)
1. Wet hands with warm water
2. Apply soap to create lather
3. Rub hands together for 15-30 seconds
4. Rinse thoroughly with water
5. Dry hands with disposable towel
6. Use towel to turn off faucet

## Special Considerations

### Artificial Nails and Jewelry
- Natural nails kept short
- Artificial nails not permitted
- Jewelry minimized (wedding band only)
- Nail polish kept intact

### Hand Dermatitis
- Moisturizing lotion usage
- Allergic reaction reporting
- Alternative products consideration
- Protective glove usage

## Product Selection and Placement

### Product Types
- Alcohol-based hand rub (60-95% alcohol)
- Antimicrobial soap
- Plain soap (for patient bathing)
- Moisturizing lotion

### Dispenser Placement
- At room entrances and exits
- Beside each patient bed
- In medication preparation areas
- At sinks and handwashing stations
