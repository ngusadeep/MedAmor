# SOP: Clinical Handoff and Continuity of Care (Sample)

## 1. Handoff Definition

- **Handoff** occurs when care responsibility shifts: between providers, between sites (e.g. primary care to specialist, ED to inpatient), or between encounters where a different clinician is responsible for follow-up.

## 2. Required Elements

- **Active problems and medications** relevant to the handoff must be summarized or referenced.
- **Pending actions** (e.g. medication review due, referral for dental care, colonoscopy follow-up) must be clearly stated.
- **Responsible provider** for the next step must be identifiable from the record (e.g. PCP, specialist, nursing).

## 3. Continuity Failures (Audit Targets)

- **Medication review due** remains active with no documented review or plan within a reasonable window.
- **Referral** (e.g. patient referral for dental care) with no subsequent encounter or documented outcome.
- **Screening** (e.g. colonoscopy, depression screening) indicated by guideline or prior note with no documented completion or deferral.
- **Provider/site change** with no handoff note or summary (e.g. urgent care visit with different provider; next visit at primary site with no reference to urgent care).

## 4. Documentation Standards

- Handoff notes may be a dedicated "handoff" or "transition of care" note, or a structured section in the progress note.
- Timeline must show logical continuity: e.g. condition resolved only after an intervention is documented; follow-up dates align with "next expected" in prior notes.

## 5. Audit Triggers

- Condition "medication review due (situation) (active)" with no subsequent medication reconciliation or review note.
- Procedure "patient referral for dental care" with no subsequent dental encounter or documented decline.
- Visit at a different location/provider with no reference in the next primary-care note to the outside encounter.
