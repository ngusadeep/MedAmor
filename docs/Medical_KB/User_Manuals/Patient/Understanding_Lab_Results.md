## Introduction
This guide helps you understand your laboratory test results and what they mean for your health.

- Request printed copies if preferred

## Understanding Result Formats

### Reference Ranges
- **Normal Range**: Most healthy people's results fall here
- **High/Low**: Results outside normal range
- **Critical Values**: Extremely abnormal results requiring immediate attention
- **Units**: Measurement units (mg/dL, mmol/L, etc.)

### Result Flags
- **H**: High (above normal range)
- **L**: Low (below normal range)
- **A**: Abnormal
- **C**: Critical (requires immediate attention)

## Common Blood Tests

### Complete Blood Count (CBC)
- **White Blood Cells (WBC)**: Infection fighting cells
  - Normal: 4,500-11,000 cells/μL
  - High: Possible infection or inflammation
  - Low: May indicate immune system issues

- **Red Blood Cells (RBC)**: Oxygen-carrying cells
  - Normal: 4.5-5.9 million cells/μL (men), 4.1-5.1 million (women)
  - Low: Anemia or blood loss
  - High: Dehydration or lung disease

- **Hemoglobin (Hgb)**: Oxygen-carrying protein
  - Normal: 14-18 g/dL (men), 12-16 g/dL (women)
  - Low: Anemia
  - High: Dehydration or lung disease

### Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP)
- **Glucose**: Blood sugar level
  - Normal: 70-99 mg/dL (fasting)
  - High: Diabetes or stress
  - Low: Insulin overdose or fasting too long

- **Creatinine**: Kidney function measure
  - Normal: 0.7-1.3 mg/dL
  - High: Kidney damage or dehydration
  - Low: Usually not concerning

- **Liver Enzymes (ALT/AST)**: Liver health indicators
  - Normal: ALT < 40 IU/L, AST < 40 IU/L
  - High: Liver damage or inflammation

## Lipid Panel

### Cholesterol Levels
- **Total Cholesterol**: Overall cholesterol level
  - Desirable: < 200 mg/dL
  - Borderline: 200-239 mg/dL
  - High: ≥ 240 mg/dL

- **LDL Cholesterol**: "Bad" cholesterol
  - Optimal: < 100 mg/dL
  - Near optimal: 100-129 mg/dL
  - Borderline: 130-159 mg/dL
  - High: 160-189 mg/dL
  - Very High: ≥ 190 mg/dL

- **HDL Cholesterol**: "Good" cholesterol
  - Poor: < 40 mg/dL (men), < 50 mg/dL (women)
  - Better: 40-59 mg/dL
  - Best: ≥ 60 mg/dL

## Thyroid Function Tests

### TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone)
- Normal: 0.4-4.0 mIU/L
- High: Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid)
- Low: Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid)

### Free T4
- Normal: 0.8-1.8 ng/dL
- High: Hyperthyroidism
- Low: Hypothyroidism

## Urinalysis

### Common Findings
- **Protein**: Normally absent or trace
  - Present: Kidney disease or infection

- **Glucose**: Normally absent
  - Present: Diabetes or high blood sugar

- **Ketones**: Normally absent
  - Present: Diabetes, starvation, or low-carb diets

- **Blood**: Normally absent
  - Present: Kidney stones, infection, or injury

## When to Contact Your Provider

### Immediate Contact
- Critical values flagged on your results
- Severe symptoms (chest pain, difficulty breathing)
- Signs of infection (fever, chills)
- Unusual bleeding or bruising

### Follow-up Needed
- Results outside normal range
- Questions about what results mean
- Concerns about treatment effectiveness
- Need for additional testing

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

### About Your Results
- What do these results mean for my health?
- Do I need any additional tests?
- Are there any lifestyle changes I should make?
- When should I have these tests repeated?

### About Your Treatment
- How do these results affect my treatment plan?
- Are my current medications working?
- Do I need to make any medication changes?
- What are the next steps in my care?
